The rain has returned! Never thought I’d miss it, but I absolutely did. Work is busy at the moment, so the slightly cooler weather has come at an ideal time. I can finally get back in the office up at the top and find some focus.

Just a few more weeks until [my next half marathon](https://www.runshoreditch.com/), so training is in full swing. I made a few changes this time round. First, I outsourced my training plan to [Runna](https://www.runna.com/) which has definitely improved my pace. Then I picked up some natural gels from [SAP](https://sapgoodenergy.com/), which are surprisingly tasty and seem to do the trick. My legs are kinda tired now, so I’m looking forward to the taper and getting this race done.

As per usual, here are my highlights from the past month, a few good links from around the web and what I have planned for the month ahead.

## Highlights from last month

The solar eclipse on the 12th was great fun. R and I built a pin hole projector with an old shoe box which worked surprisingly well. Still, I’d love to see one at actual size through solar glasses, that must be pretty special.

Spent time in Munich with a friend, wandered around [Englischer Garten](/now/26-08-22/), [watched the surfers at Eisbachwelle](/now/26-08-21/), ate Bavarian food at [Xaver’s](https://xaver-s.de/), went for a picnic and found some great places to run along the Isar.

Back in London I discovered [Hausu](https://www.hausulondon.co.uk/) and [Druid Street Saunas](https://www.druidstreetsaunas.co.uk/). Had [a brilliant night out at Fold](https://harrycresswell.com/now/26-08-10/). Went swimming in [the mixed ponds](https://www.hampsteadheath.net/swimming-ponds) over at Hampsted Heath. And watched a friend play 80s classics on his saxophone in the [Pelton Arms](https://www.peltonarms.com/).

Celebrated my brother’s 28th birthday at [Blacklock](https://theblacklock.com/) in Soho.

## Elsewhere on the web

[The Blockbuster Chronicles](https://brandons-journal.com/categories/the-blockbuster-chronicles) from Brandon’s Journal.

Really enjoying Brandon’s fantastic series about his time working at Blockbuster Video in the early 2000’s. If you were also navigating life as a teenager around this time then you’ll appreciate this a lot. Make sure you start with the [Introduction](https://brandons-journal.com/post/the-blockbuster-chronicles-introduction).

[Battling Link Rot on a Hugo Site](https://chriswiegman.com/2026/08/battling-link-rot-on-a-hugo-site/) from Chris Wiegman.

Chris shares his process for programmatically checking broken links and generating a report that can be used to fix them.

[Re-defining the IndieWeb – Focus on the philosophy, not on tools](https://vhbelvadi.com/re-defining-the-indieweb) from Venkatram Harish Belvadi.

> “_The IndieWeb is a space comprising websites whose domain ownership and organisational, operational and editorial sovereignty remain with an individual or non-corporate group of individuals, free from external obligations that compromise that sovereignty._
> 
> This definition frees up the IndieWeb from tools and platforms and protocols, and it ensures individuals have the freedom to approach their websites as they like so long as they uphold the core tenets of the IndieWeb.”

[Favicons to go](https://rknight.me/blog/favicons-to-go/) from Robb Knight.

Rob has made a rather nice favicon generator that’s entirely client side, saves to local storage and isn’t riddled with ads. What’s not to like.

[Eclipse 2026](https://aegir.org/words/eclipse-2026) from Aegir Hallmundur.

Aegir shares his eclipse experience and how he managed to capture these awesome photos with solar film, a polarising filter and a DSLR camera.

[How to distort text with SVG filters](https://henry.codes/writing/how-to-distort-text-with-svg/) from Henry Desroches.

An oldie but a goodie. I dug this one out as I keep bumping into websites with distorted type. For example, the brilliant [Cheap.Urls.Loan](https://cheap.urls.loan/), cleverly styled to look like a bi-fold leaflet by Elijah Beaton. And [Endtimes](https://endtimes.dev/), where Nathaniel uses the same technique to style headings.

Voting is now open for the [Tiny Awards 2026](https://tinyawards.net/).

> “The Tiny Awards exist because we thought it was important to shine a spotlight on the sorts of personal web projects that tend to get overlooked by more traditional, larger or more commercial awards.”

I discovered this initiative via [Manu](https://manuelmoreale.com/thoughts/tiny-awards-2026-edition) and will definitely be casting a vote.
