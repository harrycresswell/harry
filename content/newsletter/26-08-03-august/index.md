It’s drier than a tinderbox in London right now. We’re officially in drought conditions, with a hosepipe ban in place and no rain forecast any time soon. The [driest (and sunniest) July for England and Wales since records began 190 years ago](https://www.bbc.co.uk/weather/articles/c17199dy4yxo), and we still have two months of the summer left to go!

On a more positive note, it feels rather like how I remember school summer holidays. Where every day was warm and sunny and we spent most of the time outside, playing in the garden.  I’m sure we probably had some rain too, but that’s how I remember it.

As per usual, here are my highlights from the past month, a few good links from around the web and what I have planned for the month ahead.

## Highlights from last month

Picked up an SUV at Harry Reid airport and drove around the Mojave desert with a couple of friends. Spent time in Henderson, Nelson and Boulder City where we [saw the Hoover Dam](/now/26-07-13/). 

Drove to Springdale Utah for a couple of days [hiking in the red rock canyons of Zion National Park](/now/26-07-16/).

Drove [back to Las Vegas](/now/26-07-22/) for a stag-do, held my own at [TopGolf](https://topgolf.com/us/las-vegas/), watched the world cup final at [Tailgate Beach Club](https://www.tailgatebeachclub.com/) and lost all my money in a variety of hotel casinos.

Fantastic trip. I always leave the States feeling like I’ve barely scratched the surface. Hopefully I’ll be back soon.

Watched Nolan’s [The Odyssey](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt33764258/) at the Greenwich IMAX with a few friends and really enjoyed it. The cinema itself is worth a miss, however. The screen was making a strange rattling sound throughout the entire film. Kinda annoying.

Redesigned my studio website...again. Having already designed and built two versions then scrapped them for various reasons, I think it’s finally heading in the right direction. As is often the case, I’m pretty much back where I started. Simple, stripped back, something to build upon. 

## Elsewhere on the web

[Trying out an EU-based stack for this website](https://fershad.com/writing/euro-stack/) from Fershad Irani.

Enjoyed this one from Fershad. I’ve also been looking into moving some of my personal projects to EU hosted services, so I’ll definitely be bookmarking this one. 

[Before I go: Double your business with companies you like](https://daverupert.com/2026/07/number-chart-go-up/) From Dave Rupert.

Dave makes a case for doubling your support for small businesses and local alternatives who deserve your business.

[Security versus Privacy](https://ldstephens.net/posts/security-versus-privacy/) from Loren Stephens

Loren makes the distinction between security and privacy and why one should be considered before the other. He also covers how to layer services from Google and Apple to gain security from one company and privacy from another.

[From human hands.](https://mass-driver.com/article/from-human-hands) from Rutherford Craze.

> “When we turn design over to generative AI, we remove the human element from the process. When we ask Midjourney to draw an ox, it does not think of home. ChatGPT will never invent [a new calligraphy technique,](https://undercase.xyz/custom/a-guide-to-sinistral-hand) because it has no wrists to feel uncomfortable. A tool that shields us from the friction of the work is compelling, but if we don’t experience the friction, we will never change the work.
> 
> The writing we use, the very substrate of our culture, is the result of a process older than we can comprehend. Who are we to decide that process is finished? That the time of invention and human input is done, and every letter created from now on should be a remix of what came before?
> 
> AI tools perceive reality as a finite set of data, up-to-date as of about 2021: the shadows on the wall of Plato’s Cave. Right now they are very good at making shadow puppets, but they cannot see the world outside.

Rutherford explains why they don’t use AI in their design or production processes at Mass-Driver.

[Accessibility is resistance!](https://tink.uk/accessibility-is-resistance/) from Léonie Watson.

> “If you want to resist AI, make accessibility part of everything you do, every decision you make, every product you design and build. Remove the need for people to use AI to compensate, and remember, accessibility is resistance.”

This is a fantastic read from Léonie about how AI is often the only way for some people to exist independently in a largely inaccessible digital world.
